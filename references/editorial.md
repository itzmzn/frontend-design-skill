# Editorial & Institutional

Editorial and institutional interfaces should convey authority, cultural context, and reading comfort. Their identity comes from typography, proportion, imagery, and careful information hierarchy rather than decorative UI chrome.

## Visual character

Two useful directions are archival warmth—paper-like neutrals, charcoal text, restrained mineral accents—and contemporary gallery clarity—white space, deep ink, fine gray rules, and quiet captions. Accent colors such as terracotta, lapis, bronze, or forest can identify sections or active states, but should remain sparse.

Use generous negative space and fine dividers. Let artworks, documents, photography, and text blocks feel curated rather than packed into a generic card grid.

## Typography and reading

Pair a characterful editorial serif for major titles with a highly legible serif or restrained sans for body copy. Captions, accession numbers, dates, and publication metadata can use smaller tracked text. Long-form prose should normally stay near a 65–75 character measure with relaxed leading.

Drop caps, pull quotes, footnotes, and margin notes are appropriate when they strengthen the publication language; they should not interrupt reading merely for decoration.

## Common layouts

An institutional landing page can pair a major exhibition or program image with title, dates, credits, and a clear visit/ticket action. Operational information—today's hours, admission, location, accessibility—should be easy to find without competing with the curatorial story.

Object or accession pages should give the artwork generous visual space and organize metadata as a readable definition list: accession number, maker, dates, medium, dimensions, provenance, acquisition, and related references. Zoom or high-resolution viewing should be discoverable where useful.

Editorial homepages need visible salience tiers: one dominant lead story, a smaller group of secondary features, and compact archive/department rows. Long essays can use an asymmetric main column with margin notes, figure references, citations, or archival thumbnails aligned to the relevant passage.

Exhibition narratives can use chapter navigation or a subtle reading-progress rail. Numbering should feel editorial—“01. Introduction”—rather than like fake code syntax.

## Captions, citations, and figures

Place captions directly with their image and use a consistent figure-numbering system. Footnote markers should link to readable notes or margin references. Pull quotes should be typographically distinct but should not repeat so often that they fragment the essay.

## Responsive behavior

On mobile, protect reading measure and image scale. Move margin notes into inline callouts or end notes, collapse complex metadata grids into stacked definitions, and keep operational information accessible without turning the top of the page into a dense utility panel.

## Avoid

Avoid generic SaaS cards, excessive rounded containers, tiny low-contrast captions, decorative archival labels with no meaning, and layouts that make operational information difficult to locate. Do not sacrifice text readability for an art-directed composition.
