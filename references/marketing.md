# Landing & Marketing

Marketing pages should communicate a specific promise quickly, prove it, and lead the visitor toward an appropriate next action. Distinctive art direction is useful only when the value proposition remains understandable.

## Use this reference for

Product launches, startup landing pages, campaigns, service sites, event pages, pricing pages, waitlists, and conversion-focused product storytelling.

## Page narrative

A strong sequence often moves from promise → evidence → mechanism → proof → decision. The hero should establish what the product is, who it is for, and why it matters, with one dominant CTA and an optional secondary path of lower visual weight.

Follow with real product evidence: interface previews, demonstrations, outcomes, customer proof, or concrete capabilities. Feature sections should show how the product works rather than repeating abstract adjectives.

Pricing should appear after enough context exists to understand the offer. Plans need audience, price, billing cadence, important limits, and a clear action. FAQs should resolve real objections rather than pad page length.

## Copy and CTAs

Prefer specific verbs and outcomes over generic “Learn more,” “Unlock,” or “Transform” language. Keep headline and supporting copy complementary instead of saying the same thing twice. A CTA label should describe the next step accurately.

Do not place several equally loud buttons in the same viewport. Repeat the primary action at natural decision points, not after every paragraph.

## Composition

Use asymmetric layouts, split compositions, editorial grids, or bento structures only when they establish hierarchy. Bento is not a default: each cell should have a distinct purpose and the overall grid must remain coherent on smaller screens.

Alternate dense explanation with visual breathing room. Avoid a long sequence of identical icon-card sections. Let product media, typography, diagrams, or photography create rhythm.

## Typography and color

Choose display type with enough personality to establish the campaign and pair it with highly readable body type. Use a restrained palette with a deliberate accent. Gradients, glow, glass, and oversized type are valid when they support the brand; they should not appear automatically because the page is “modern.”

## Image briefs

Generate campaign imagery with a defined subject, environment, lighting, framing, and empty space for copy. Product screenshots or diagrams should remain truthful to the interface. Avoid generic abstract AI blobs, random 3D spheres, and unrelated futuristic backgrounds.

## Avoid

Avoid vague superlatives, fabricated logos or testimonials, endless feature-card grids, three competing hero CTAs, autoplay motion that delays comprehension, and pricing tables that hide important limits. The page should still make sense with decorative effects removed.
