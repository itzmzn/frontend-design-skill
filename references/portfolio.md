# Portfolio & Photography

Portfolio design should make the work memorable and easy to inspect. The interface is a frame for the creator's output, not a competing showcase of UI tricks.

## Use this reference for

Photography, art direction, architecture, design, development case studies, student work, team showcases, creative studios, and other media-led personal or collective portfolios.

## Media-first layouts

Use grids, masonry, editorial sequences, or controlled bento compositions according to the work. Preserve image ratios when they carry artistic intent; crop only when the presentation system requires consistency and the subject remains intact.

A gallery should establish rhythm through scale and spacing rather than wrapping every image in a card. Provide a clear path from overview to project detail and make image enlargement or lightbox behavior predictable.

## Hero and identity

A split hero can pair a strong portrait/project image with concise identity, role, location or specialty, and one useful next action. Oversized typography can work when it does not bury the work or force essential information below several screens of decorative type.

Personal identity should feel specific. Avoid generic “creative developer crafting digital experiences” copy when concrete disciplines, tools, clients, or project outcomes can communicate more.

## Case studies

A case study should establish context, role, constraints, process, key decisions, final work, and outcome. Use visuals at the point where they support the narrative. Do not create a gallery of attractive mockups with no explanation of what was solved.

Student or team directories should keep portraits, names, roles, and links consistent while allowing individual work to remain distinctive.

## Typography and color

Let typography support the creator's discipline. Editorial serif pairings can suit photography and architecture; expressive grotesques can suit fashion or experimental work; restrained sans-serif systems can keep multidisciplinary portfolios neutral. Keep the palette subordinate to the artwork unless color is itself part of the identity.

## Image briefs

When generating supporting imagery, specify editorial genre, lens/camera distance, lighting, environment, wardrobe or materials, and aspect ratio. Do not generate fake client work or imply authorship of work the portfolio owner did not create.

## Avoid

Avoid autoplay galleries that remove user control, overdesigned loaders, cursor gimmicks that impair navigation, low-contrast captions, inconsistent project thumbnails, and long intro animations before the work becomes accessible.
